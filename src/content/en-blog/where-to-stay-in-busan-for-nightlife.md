If clubbing is the main reason you are going out in Busan, book your hotel in **Seomyeon**, the city's downtown club street. Pick **Haeundae** if you want a beach hotel and plan only one or two club nights, and **Gwangalli** if you want bridge views and bars more than clubs. The deciding factor is distance: Busan's subway stops around midnight, and the clubs keep going until morning.

## Why distance matters more in Busan than in Seoul

Visit Busan lists the Busan Metro as running from 05:00 to 23:59, with the last train depending on the station. It also notes there is no separate night bus. Most Busan clubs run on Fridays and Saturdays until early morning, so the ride home from a club is almost always a taxi, unless you can walk.

Busan's three nightlife areas are also spread out along one subway line. On Line 2, Gwangan is 10 stops from Seomyeon and Haeundae is 16 stops from Seomyeon. That is a fine trip at 8 p.m., but not one you can make by subway at 4 a.m.

## Seomyeon: stay here for the clubs

Seomyeon is Busan's club street and its largest downtown. Visit Busan describes it as crowded day and night, with shopping, Samjung Tower and Seomyeon Market's food alley. Busan clubs feel more relaxed than Seoul's, entry is usually ₩15,000–30,000, and the crowd is mostly local. Our [Busan clubs](/en/clubs/busan) page lists them all.

Three Seomyeon clubs can be booked through NightFlow:

- **Groove & Spot:** open nightly, hip-hop and EDM, buy one drink to enter.
- **BELPOS:** Fridays and Saturdays, no slippers.
- **Azit:** Thursday to Sunday, opens around 1 a.m., ₩20,000 entry. A late second stop.

**Where to stay:** near Seomyeon Station, where Lines 1 and 2 meet. Line 2 also takes you to Gwangan and Haeundae for the beaches, and from Gimhae Airport the light rail connects to Line 2 at Sasang.

**Watch out for:** most clubs run Friday and Saturday only. If you are in Busan Sunday to Thursday, Groove & Spot is the only bookable club open every night, and Azit adds Thursdays and Sundays.

## Haeundae: beach first, clubs second

Haeundae Beach is the best-known beach in Korea, and Busan's official guide puts it about five minutes on foot from Haeundae Station (Line 2, exit 3 or 5). Haeundae has beach-front and hotel clubs with a table culture, and they are busiest in summer beach season.

**Where to stay:** between Haeundae Station and the beach. You get the beach on your doorstep, but on a Seomyeon club night you are 16 stops away, and the trip home is a taxi.

**Evenings without clubs:** this is where Haeundae is strong. Ride the Blue Line Park beach train before sunset (₩8,000–10,000 a ride, and it stops running at about 19:30 in October), eat at Haeundae Market, and walk out after dark for the Marine City skyline from The Bay 101. Our [Haeundae evening](/en/night-activities/haeundae-evening) route has the details.

## Gwangalli: views and bars, the middle option

Visit Busan calls Gwangalli the beach closest to the city center and a meeting place for young locals. It has restaurants, sea-view cafés and water sports such as paddleboarding and windsurfing, and the Gwangandaegyo Bridge is lit up at night. The Busan International Fireworks Festival is held here every year.

Every Saturday there is a free drone show over the beach, about 10–12 minutes long and held twice a night: 19:00 and 21:00 from October to February, 20:00 and 22:00 from March to September. Weather can cancel it, so check the official site that day.

**Where to stay:** near Gwangan Station (Line 2), about a 13-minute walk from exit 5 to the beach, or Geumnyeonsan Station on the same line. Gwangalli has no clubs on our list, but it is 10 stops from Seomyeon instead of 16. A good Saturday plan is the 21:00 drone show, then Seomyeon for the clubs ([Busan night route](/en/night-activities/busan-night)).

## Getting back to your hotel at night

- **Subway:** ends around midnight (05:00 to 23:59 according to Visit Busan). Check the exact last train from your station on the day.
- **Taxi:** since June 2023, Busan taxis charge a late-night surcharge from 23:00 to 04:00: 30% from midnight to 2 a.m. and 20% in the other hours. A ride home after 2 a.m. costs less than one at 1 a.m.
- **Bus:** there is no separate night bus, and regular buses add an extra charge after 23:40.
- **Set up first:** install a taxi app before you go out, carry some cash, and save 1330 (Korea Travel Helpline). See [getting around at night](/en/night-activities/getting-around).

## Which area should you pick?

1. **Going out Friday and Saturday, clubs are the point:** Seomyeon. You walk home.
2. **Beach holiday with one club night:** Haeundae, and take a taxi back from Seomyeon that night.
3. **You want night views and bars, and maybe one club:** Gwangalli. It is closer to Seomyeon than Haeundae is.
4. **Summer trip and you want beach clubs:** Haeundae, where the club scene peaks in the beach season.

If you are booking a Seomyeon table, book it before the hotel so your dates line up with the Friday and Saturday club nights. Clubs are 19+, so bring your physical passport ([entry rules](/en/club-entry-rules)), plus ₩20,000–30,000 in cash in case the door terminal refuses a foreign card.
